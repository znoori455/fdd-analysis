What to keep in mind

  • Medical practices (dental, physician, optometry) had low defaults in every decade, but most states require a licensed clinician to own them.
  • Self-storage had the lowest risk on the list, with no defaults among purchase loans. It is mostly real estate, with a median loan near $860K in the 2010s.
  • Car washes, collision repair and plumbing/HVAC are about average when startups are included, but purchases of established businesses did much better. Car washes were hit hard in the recession and need heavy capital. Plumbing and HVAC share one industry code.
  • Oil change & lube is about average across all loans, including through the recession. Purchases of existing shops did better, but on only 62 loans. Electric vehicles, which need no oil changes, are a long-term risk over a 10-year loan.
  • Commercial cleaning, electrical and property management were average or worse. Residential property management had the highest recession-era default rate on the list.
  • Property restoration has no industry code of its own, so remediation services stands in for it. See the Franchise brands tab for SERVPRO and other restoration brands.
  • Industry averages mix franchised and independent businesses, and purchase samples are small. The quality of the specific business and your cash reserve matter more.

SBA 504 loans: which businesses held up, 1991–2019

504 loans are a separate SBA program from the 7(a) loans on the other tabs. They finance owner-occupied real estate and heavy equipment, so borrowers are usually established businesses buying their building. Loans approved 1991–2009 went through the 2008 recession and are the real stress test. Loans approved 2010–2019 have had seven or more years to play out. Loans approved since 2020 are left out because they are too young to judge. This tab already includes 504 loans from 1991, so the 1990s switch doesn't change it. The risk index is actual charge-offs ÷ the charge-offs expected at the all-504 average for each approval year: 1.0 = average, 0.5 = half as many.

Method and sources

  • Default means the SBA loan was charged off. Cancelled and undisbursed loans are excluded. For loans made 2000–2019, only resolved loans (paid in full or charged off) are counted. Loans made since 2020 are counted as charge-offs to date ÷ all disbursed loans; most defaults arrive around year four, so these are floors.
  • Risk index = a brand's actual defaults ÷ the defaults expected if it had matched the all-loan average in each decade (23.9% for 2000s loans, 8.0% for 2010s loans, 2.5% so far for 2020s loans).
  • Brands were matched by name in the SBA FranchiseName field (for example, "Midas" and "Midas Muffler Shop" count together). Lenders enter these labels and they are incomplete, so counts can differ slightly from other sites.
  • The 2000–2009 SBA file has no change-of-ownership flag, so the loan-size chart and the industry purchase risk use 2010 onward only.
  • All industries groups loans by the industry description on each loan, merging names the SBA changed in 2022 (for example "Liquor Stores" became "Liquor Retailers"). About 17,000 loans from the 2000s have no industry code and are left out. Its averages (24.1% for 2000s loans, 8.0% for 2010s loans) cover only loans with an industry code, so the 2000s figure is slightly higher than the 23.9% shown on the Franchise brands tab.
  • Industries are grouped by the NAICS code on each loan (for example 531130 self-storage, 621210 dentists, 238220 plumbing & HVAC). Purchase risk compares defaults on change-of-ownership loans made 2010–2023 with the average for all such loans (7.3% for 2010s loans, 2.0% so far for 2020–23 loans).
  • 1990s loans (switch at the top): SBA 7(a) loans approved FY1991–1999, counted the same way as the 2000s file (cancelled and undisbursed loans out; resolved loans only). They add a 1990s column and fold into each risk and safety index at the 1990s all-loan average. Brands are matched by the 1990s FranchiseName spellings (for example "SUBWAY SANDWICH SHOP", "MIDAS MUFFLER SHOP"). Industry codes are on only about 45% of 1990s loans and skew toward some trades, so the 1990s industry figures are thinner than other decades. The loan-size chart and purchase risk stay 2010 onward, since the 1990s file has no change-of-ownership flag.
  • SBA 504 loans tab: built from the SBA 504 FOIA files for FY1991–2009 and FY2010–present (statuses as of September 30, 2026), counting funded loans approved through FY2019; cancelled and unfunded loans are excluded. Loans whose status the SBA marks "exempt" (mostly active loans) count as not charged off. Its risk index uses each approval year's all-504 charge-off rate as the expectation. Industry codes changed between NAICS editions are merged (for example 722110 into 722511, full-service restaurants).
